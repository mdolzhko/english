When one verb follows another, the second one takes a fixed form, and the
first verb decides which. There is no rule that covers every verb, but a few
patterns do most of the work:

    ["verb + object + to", "tell, ask, want, advise, allow, remind, persuade…", "She told me [to wait]. I want you [to listen]."],
    ["modal verbs and let, make", "bare infinitive, no to", "You must [go]. Let me [see]. They made us [wait]."],
    ["as the subject", "-ing", "[Smoking] is bad for you. [Learning] a language takes time."],

<Note label="A test for the mixed verbs">
If the second action is still ahead when the first verb happens — remember to
lock, stop to smoke, try to open — it is the to-infinitive. If it is already
behind, or is the activity itself — remember locking, stop smoking, try
opening — it is -ing.
</Note>
