## Topic

Beyond forming tenses, auxiliary verbs carry several jobs in conversation. All of
them reuse the auxiliary already present in the other person’s sentence — and if
there is no auxiliary, *do / does / did* stands in for it.

**Echo questions** show interest or surprise: they repeat the auxiliary and keep
the same polarity as the statement. *I had good marks.* → *Did you?* (no
auxiliary in the statement, so *did*). *I wouldn’t do that.* → *Wouldn’t you?*
(negative statement, negative echo).

**Short answers** repeat the auxiliary from the question, not the main verb, and
are never contracted in the positive. *Were they living in London?* → *Yes, they
were.* *Is it OK?* → *Yes, it is* (not *yes, it’s*).

**Emphatic do** contradicts or insists, and is stressed in speech. *She doesn’t
eat meat.* → *She **does** eat meat.* The main verb stays in the infinitive.

**Question tags** reverse the polarity of the statement: positive statement →
negative tag, and the other way round. *We should finish, shouldn’t we?* /
*You aren’t ready, are you?* Two patterns worth memorising separately: *Let’s ...*
takes *shall we?*, and an imperative takes *will you?*

**Neither / So** invert as well: *Neither have I*, *So am I* — auxiliary before
the subject. Note that after *neither* the auxiliary is positive, because
*neither* already carries the negation.
