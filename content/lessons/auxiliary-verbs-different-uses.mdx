## Topic

Beyond forming tenses, auxiliary verbs carry four jobs in conversation. All of
them reuse the auxiliary already present in the other person's sentence — and if
there is no auxiliary, *do / does / did* stands in for it.

**Echo questions** show interest or surprise: they repeat the auxiliary and keep
the same polarity as the statement. *I had good marks.* → *Did you?* (no
auxiliary in the statement, so *did*). *I wouldn't do that.* → *Wouldn't you?*
(negative statement, negative echo).

**Short answers** repeat the auxiliary from the question, not the main verb.
*Were they living in London?* → *Yes, they were.*

**Emphatic do** contradicts or insists, and is stressed in speech. *She doesn't
eat meat.* → *She **does** eat meat.* The main verb stays in the infinitive.

**Question tags** reverse the polarity of the statement: positive statement →
negative tag. *We should finish, shouldn't we?* One exception worth memorising:
*Let's ...* always takes *shall we?*

**Neither / So** invert as well: *Neither have I*, *So do I* — auxiliary before
the subject.
