*The* tells the listener that they know which one you mean. When they cannot
know, or when you mean things in general, there is no article. Most of the
lesson is lists of which names take it and which do not, so the cards are
made to be looked up rather than read in one go. The notes follow English
with Lucy’s *Everything to know about ‘the’*, regrouped.

<Note label="The sound, not the letter">
*The university* is /ðə/, because *university* starts with /j/; *the hour* is
/ðiː/, because the *h* is silent. It is the same choice as between *a* and *an*.
</Note>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="specific"
  title="One the listener can pick out"
  form="the + noun"
  chips={["already mentioned", "only one", "superlatives", "first … last"]}
  gist="The listener knows which one: it came up before, a description picks it out, or there is only one. Superlatives, only and first … last pick out one by themselves."
  hint="The goes with countable and uncountable nouns alike, whenever the listener knows which one is meant."
>

*The* says the listener knows which one you mean. It goes with countable and
uncountable nouns alike — *the cars*, *the coffee* — singular or plural.

<Rule id="already-mentioned" title="Already mentioned" hint="A when something first comes up, the once the listener knows which one: a bike → the bike.">

- *I just got **a** new sofa. **The** sofa is blue.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="described" title="Picked out by a description" hint="A description that tells which one makes the noun specific: the broken table — the one we both know is broken.">

- *Don’t put your phone on **the** broken table.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="unique" title="The only one there is" hint="Something there is only one of: the sun, the moon, the sky.">

- ***The** sun is out today!*

</Rule>

<Rule id="superlatives" title="Superlatives" hint="the + superlative: the best, the slowest, the most interesting — and one of the most …">

- *We had **the** best time yesterday.*
- *This has to be **the** slowest taxi in town.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="only" title="The only" hint="the only + noun: the only person, the only bowl.">

- *My aunt is **the** only person I trust.*
- *Is this **the** only bowl you have?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="ordinals" title="First, second … last" hint="Ordinal numbers, and last, pick out one: the first thing, the second time, the last time.">

- *This is **the** second time she’s been late.*
- *You are **the** fifth doctor I’ve talked to about this.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="whole-kind" title="A whole kind, in the singular" hint="the + singular noun for every one of its kind: the wolf is dangerous. Instruments too: play the piano, the guitar.">

- ***The** wolf is a dangerous animal.* — wolves in general, not one wolf.
- *My cousin plays **the** violin.* — the instrument, not a particular violin.

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="names-with-the"
  title="Names that take ‘the’"
  form="the + name"
  chips={["plural or ‘republic’ countries", "regions", "seas, rivers, ranges"]}
  gist="Most names go without an article, but some kinds take the: plural countries and kingdoms or republics, regions, seas, rivers, mountain ranges, famous buildings, hotels, newspapers and organisations."
>

<Rule id="plural-countries" title="Plural countries, kingdoms, republics and states" hint="A country takes the when its name is plural or says what kind of state it is: the Netherlands, the UK, the United States, the Dominican Republic.">

- *Have you been to **the** Netherlands?*
- *The beaches in **the** Bahamas are incredible!*
- *She is currently in **the** Dominican Republic.*
- *Where should I go in **the** United States?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="regions" title="Regions and compass directions" hint="the north, the south of France, the Middle East, the equator. With an adjective there is none: northern Canada.">

- *I’ve never been to **the** north of France.*
- ***The** south is quite warm this time of year.*
- *She was born in **the** Middle East.*
- *The best coffee grows near **the** equator.*

<Note label="Not “the North Canada”">
Either *the north of Canada* or *northern Canada*. The adjective form takes no
article, and the country stays without one either way.
</Note>

</Rule>

<Rule id="buildings" title="Famous buildings and works of art" hint="the Eiffel Tower, the Louvre, the Mona Lisa.">

- *Did you go to Paris just to see **the** Eiffel Tower?*
- *I should have seen **the** Mona Lisa.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="hotels" title="Hotels, restaurants and pubs" hint="the Hilton, the Ritz, the Ship and Shovel — but none when the name starts with a person’s name: Froggy’s Tavern.">

- *Don’t stay at **the** Ritz-Carlton; you should stay at **the** Hilton!*
- *Let’s have a few pints at **the** Ship and Shovel.*

But no article when the name starts with someone’s name: *We should head down
to Froggy’s Tavern.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="newspapers" title="Newspapers" hint="the Guardian, the Times.">

- *Mandy told me to read this article from **the** Guardian.*
- *We haven’t read the review in **the** Times yet.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="organisations" title="Organisations and initialisms" hint="the World Health Organization, the US Postal Service, and names read letter by letter: the FBI, the BBC, the UK. A short name said as a word has none: NATO.">

- *She works for **the** US Postal Service.*
- ***The** World Health Organization just mentioned something about that.*
- ***The** FBI is investigating this situation.* — F-B-I, read letter by letter.

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="people-and-time"
  title="Groups of people, dates and periods"
  form="the + adjective · the + date"
  chips={["the rich", "the Smiths", "the 25th", "the 1800s"]}
  gist="An adjective for a whole group of people, a family by its surname, a date, a part of the day, and a decade or a century take the."
>

<Rule id="groups" title="A group of people, by an adjective" hint="the + adjective for everyone it describes: the rich, the poor, the young, the Spanish. The verb after it is plural.">

- *There have been recent calls to tax **the** rich.*
- ***The** Spanish settled here in 1695.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="families" title="A family, by its surname" hint="the + surname in the plural, for the whole family: the Smiths, the McAllisters.">

- ***The** McAllisters moved last year.*
- *We had dinner with **the** Smiths last night.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="dates" title="Dates and parts of the day" hint="on the 25th, in the morning, in the evening — but at night, at noon.">

- *We will celebrate Leo’s birthday on **the** 25th.*
- *Can you come in **the** morning?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="periods" title="Decades and centuries" hint="in the 1800s, in the nineties, in the 16th century.">

- *My great-grandmother was born in **the** 1800s.*
- *I wish I had lived in **the** 16th century.*

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="no-the"
  title="No ‘the’"
  form="no article"
  chips={["things in general", "most names", "subjects, sports, languages"]}
  gist="Things in general and most names — people, companies, countries, cities, continents, streets, lakes, single mountains — go without an article, and so do school subjects, sports, languages, a noun with a number and acronyms."
>

Without a reason to pick one out there is no article, and most names already
say which one they mean.

<Rule id="in-general" title="Things in general" hint="Plural and uncountable nouns about things in general take no article: apples, bread, life, money, people, taxes.">

- *She buys apples.*
- *He is baking bread.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="names" title="People, holidays and companies" hint="Names of people, holidays and companies take no article: John, Christmas, Apple, Facebook.">

- *Do you know John?*
- *They have plans for Christmas and New Year’s.*
- *I stopped using Twitter and Facebook when I got my Apple iPhone.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="place-names" title="Most place names" hint="Most countries, cities, states, continents, streets, lakes and single mountains take no article: Japan, Kyiv, Florida, Africa, Lombard Street, Lake Windermere, Hoverla.">

</Rule>

<Rule id="universities" title="Universities" hint="A university known by a place name has no article: Oxford, Stanford. One whose name starts with University takes the: the University of Miami.">

- *When Scarlet finishes her undergrad at Oxford, she will transfer to Stanford.*
- But: *My brother will be teaching at **the** University of Miami next year.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="subjects-sports" title="School subjects and sports" hint="maths, geometry, history; football, baseball — no article.">

- *My daughter is having trouble with maths.*
- *I haven’t played baseball since I was young.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="languages" title="Languages and nationalities" hint="Italian, Japanese — no article. With the word language after it, the: the Spanish language.">

- *We’ve been learning Italian since April.*
- *I just found out that I have Japanese ancestry.*
- But: *I’m studying **the** Spanish language.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="numbers" title="A noun followed by a number" hint="platform 3, room 12, gate 5 — no article.">

- *She and her husband will find you on platform 3.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="acronyms" title="Acronyms said as a word" hint="NATO, NASA, UNESCO are said as words and take no article. Letters read one by one take the: the FBI, the UK.">

- *We are still waiting to hear what NATO has to say.*
- But: ***The** FBI is investigating this situation.*

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="the-or-not"
  title="‘The’ or not"
  chips={["places we go to", "titles", "pairs that look alike"]}
  gist="Home, work, school, church and hospital go without an article when we mean what happens there; the cinema and the theatre keep it. Then the pairs that are easiest to mix up, side by side."
>

<Rule id="places" title="Home, work, school, church, hospital" hint="No article when we mean what the place is for: at home, at work, to school, to church, in hospital. Americans say in the hospital.">

- *She is on her way home.*
- *Can you pick me up from work today?*
- *What time do you need to go to school?* — and primary school, secondary school.
- *She’s in her first year of university.*
- *My friend and I met at church when we were 12.*
- *Dad’s in hospital at the moment.*

<Note label="British and American">
*In hospital* is British; in American English it is *in the hospital*.
</Note>

</Rule>

<Rule id="cinema" title="The cinema, the theatre" hint="go to the cinema, go to the theatre — these keep the, unlike school or work.">

- *Shall we go to **the** cinema tonight?*
- *We went to **the** theatre on Saturday.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="titles" title="Titles of films and books" hint="A title keeps exactly what the original has: Dune, but The Matrix and The Lord of the Rings.">

- *Have you seen Dune?*
- *I’ve read The Lord of the Rings twice.* — *The* is part of the title.

</Rule>
