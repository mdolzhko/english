*The* tells the listener that they know which one you mean. When they cannot
know, or when you mean things in general, there is no article. Most of the
lesson is lists of which names take it and which do not, so the cards are
made to be looked up rather than read in one go.

<Note label="The sound, not the letter">
*The university* is /ðə/, because *university* starts with /j/; *the hour* is
/ðiː/, because the *h* is silent. It is the same choice as between *a* and *an*.
</Note>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="specific"
  title="One the listener can pick out"
  form="the + noun"
  chips={["already mentioned", "only one", "superlatives", "first … last"]}
  gist="The listener knows which one: it came up before, a description picks it out, or there is only one. Superlatives, only and first … last pick out one by themselves."
  hint="The goes with countable and uncountable nouns alike, whenever the listener knows which one is meant."
>

*The* says the listener knows which one you mean. It goes with countable and
uncountable nouns alike — *the trains*, *the water* — singular or plural.

<Rule id="already-mentioned" title="Already mentioned" hint="A when something first comes up, the once the listener knows which one: a bike → the bike.">

- *We stayed in **a** small hotel near the station. **The** hotel had no lift.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="described" title="Picked out by a description" hint="A description that tells which one makes the noun specific: the blue folder on my desk — that one, not any folder.">

- *Can you hand me **the** blue folder on my desk?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="unique" title="The only one there is" hint="Something there is only one of: the sun, the moon, the sky.">

- ***The** moon was so bright last night.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="superlatives" title="Superlatives" hint="the + superlative: the best, the coldest, the most interesting — and one of the most …">

- *It was **the** coldest winter I can remember.*
- *Kyiv is **the** biggest city in Ukraine.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="only" title="The only" hint="the only + noun: the only person, the only train.">

- *You’re **the** only person who knows about this.*
- *Is this **the** only train to Lviv today?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="ordinals" title="First, second … last" hint="Ordinal numbers, and last, pick out one: the first thing, the third time, the last time.">

- *It’s **the** third time this week the lift has broken.*
- *Our flat is on **the** fifth floor.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="whole-kind" title="A whole kind, in the singular" hint="the + singular noun for every one of its kind: the smartphone changed how we read. Instruments too: play the piano, the guitar.">

- ***The** smartphone has changed how we read.* — smartphones in general, not one phone.
- *She’s learning to play **the** cello.* — the instrument, not a particular cello.

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="names-with-the"
  title="Names that take ‘the’"
  form="the + name"
  chips={["plural or ‘republic’ countries", "regions", "seas, rivers, ranges"]}
  gist="Most names go without an article, but some kinds take the: plural countries and kingdoms or republics, regions, seas, rivers, mountain ranges, famous buildings, hotels, newspapers and organisations."
>

<Rule id="plural-countries" title="Plural countries, kingdoms, republics and states" hint="A country takes the when its name is plural or says what kind of state it is: the Netherlands, the UK, the United States, the Czech Republic.">

- *Tulips grow everywhere in **the** Netherlands.*
- *We flew to **the** Philippines last spring.*
- *Is it cheap to travel around **the** Czech Republic?*
- *She has worked in **the** United States for ten years.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="regions" title="Regions and compass directions" hint="the north, the south of Ukraine, the Middle East, the equator. With an adjective there is none: northern Canada.">

- *It’s much warmer in **the** south of Ukraine.*
- ***The** west of the country is full of mountains.*
- *Oil prices depend a lot on **the** Middle East.*
- *Days and nights are almost equal near **the** equator.*

<Note label="Not “the North Canada”">
Either *the north of Canada* or *northern Canada*. The adjective form takes no
article, and the country stays without one either way.
</Note>

</Rule>

<Rule id="buildings" title="Famous buildings and works of art" hint="the Louvre, the Golden Gate in Kyiv, the Last Supper.">

- *We queued for an hour to get into **the** Louvre.*
- *Have you seen **the** Golden Gate in Kyiv?*
- ***The** Last Supper is much bigger than I expected.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="hotels" title="Hotels, restaurants and pubs" hint="the Hilton, the Grand Hotel, the Red Lion — but none when the name starts with a person’s name: McDonald’s, Mario’s.">

- *Let’s meet in the bar of **the** Grand Hotel.*
- *There’s a quiz night at **the** Red Lion on Thursdays.*

But no article when the name starts with someone’s name: *We had lunch at
Mario’s*, *Is there a McDonald’s near here?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="newspapers" title="Newspapers" hint="the Times, the Financial Times, the Kyiv Independent.">

- *I read about it in **the** Kyiv Independent.*
- *My boss reads **the** Financial Times every morning.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="organisations" title="Organisations and initialisms" hint="the United Nations, the European Union, and names read letter by letter: the BBC, the EU, the UK. A name said as a word has none: NASA, NATO.">

- ***The** United Nations was founded in 1945.*
- ***The** EU has twenty-seven members.*
- *She has worked for **the** BBC for years.* — B-B-C, read letter by letter.

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="people-and-time"
  title="Groups of people, dates and periods"
  form="the + adjective · the + date"
  chips={["the rich", "the Smiths", "the 14th", "the 1950s"]}
  gist="An adjective for a whole group of people, a family by its surname, a date, a part of the day, and a decade or a century take the."
>

<Rule id="groups" title="A group of people, by an adjective" hint="the + adjective for everyone it describes: the rich, the poor, the elderly, the unemployed. The verb after it is plural.">

- *The city opened a new centre for **the** elderly.*
- ***The** unemployed can get free courses there.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="families" title="A family, by its surname" hint="the + surname in the plural, for the whole family: the Smiths, the Kovalenkos.">

- *We’re spending Christmas with **the** Kovalenkos.*
- ***The** Johnsons next door have a new dog.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="dates" title="Dates and parts of the day" hint="on the 14th, in the morning, in the evening — but at night, at noon.">

- *My exam is on **the** 14th.*
- *I usually go running in **the** evening.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="periods" title="Decades and centuries" hint="in the 1950s, in the nineties, in the 12th century.">

- *My grandfather was born in **the** 1950s.*
- *This church was built in **the** 12th century.*

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="no-the"
  title="No ‘the’"
  form="no article"
  chips={["things in general", "most names", "subjects, sports, languages"]}
  gist="Things in general and most names — people, companies, countries, cities, continents, streets, lakes, single mountains — go without an article, and so do school subjects, sports, languages, a noun with a number and acronyms."
>

Without a reason to pick one out there is no article, and most names already
say which one they mean.

<Rule id="in-general" title="Things in general" hint="Plural and uncountable nouns about things in general take no article: books, coffee, life, money, people, taxes.">

- *I don’t drink coffee after six.*
- *Books are cheaper online.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="names" title="People, holidays and companies" hint="Names of people, holidays and companies take no article: Oleh, Easter, Google.">

- *Have you met Oleh?*
- *We always visit my parents at Easter.*
- *She has just started a job at Google.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="place-names" title="Most place names" hint="Most countries, cities, states, continents, streets, lakes and single mountains take no article: Japan, Kyiv, Texas, Africa, Khreshchatyk, Lake Svitiaz, Hoverla.">

</Rule>

<Rule id="universities" title="Universities" hint="A university known by a place name has no article: Oxford, Cambridge. One whose name starts with University takes the: the University of Edinburgh.">

- *She is doing a master’s at Cambridge.*
- But: *He teaches at **the** University of Edinburgh.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="subjects-sports" title="School subjects and sports" hint="maths, chemistry, history; football, tennis — no article.">

- *I was never good at chemistry.*
- *We play football every Saturday.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="languages" title="Languages and nationalities" hint="Polish, Japanese — no article. With the word language after it, the: the Ukrainian language.">

- *My grandmother speaks Polish and Hungarian.*
- *Is Japanese hard to learn?*
- But: *He wrote a book about **the** Ukrainian language.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="numbers" title="A noun followed by a number" hint="gate 12, page 40, room 5 — no article.">

- *Our flight leaves from gate 12.*
- *Open your books at page 40.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="acronyms" title="Acronyms said as a word" hint="NASA, NATO, UNESCO are said as words and take no article. Letters read one by one take the: the BBC, the UN, the UK.">

- *NASA was founded in 1958.*
- But: ***The** UN meets in New York.*

</Rule>

</Topic>

<Topic
  id="the-or-not"
  title="‘The’ or not"
  chips={["places we go to", "titles", "pairs that look alike"]}
  gist="Home, work, school, church and hospital go without an article when we mean what happens there; the cinema and the theatre keep it. Then the pairs that are easiest to mix up, side by side."
>

<Rule id="places" title="Home, work, school, church, hospital" hint="No article when we mean what the place is for: at home, at work, to school, to church, in hospital. Americans say in the hospital.">

- *I left my keys at home.*
- *She’s still at work.*
- *Children here start school at six.* — and primary school, secondary school.
- *My brother is in his second year at university.*
- *They go to church every Sunday.*
- *My grandmother was in hospital for a week.*

<Note label="British and American">
*In hospital* is British; in American English it is *in the hospital*.
</Note>

</Rule>

<Rule id="cinema" title="The cinema, the theatre" hint="go to the cinema, go to the theatre — these keep the, unlike school or work.">

- *Shall we go to **the** cinema tonight?*
- *We went to **the** theatre on Saturday.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="titles" title="Titles of films and books" hint="A title keeps exactly what the original has: Dune, but The Matrix and The Lord of the Rings.">

- *Have you seen Dune?*
- *I’ve read The Lord of the Rings twice.* — *The* is part of the title.

</Rule>
