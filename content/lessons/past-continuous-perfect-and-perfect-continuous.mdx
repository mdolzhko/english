## Past Continuous

A story’s main events go in the past simple, one after another. The past
continuous is for what was **already going on** around those events — the
background, not the plot.

<Chart
  labelColumn
  columns={["", "was / were + -ing"]}
  rows={[
    ["affirmative", "I [was working]. They [were working]."],
    ["negative", "She [wasn’t working]. We [weren’t working]."],
    ["question", "[Was] he [working]? What [were] you [doing] at 9?"],
  ]}
/>

<Rule id="in-progress" title="Actions in progress at a past moment" hint="was / were + -ing for an action that was unfinished at a specific past moment. The moment is set by a time expression or by a past simple clause.">

Use it for an action that was in progress, and not finished, at a specific
moment in the past. The moment can be a time expression (*at 9 a.m.*, *at
midday*, *all morning*) or a past simple sentence.

- *‘What were you doing at 9?’ ‘I was studying.’*
- *At midday, they were still working.*
- *They were swimming when I saw them.*
- *When she arrived, they were still working.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="setting-the-scene" title="Setting the scene" hint="A story opens with was / were + -ing to describe the situation; then the events arrive in past simple.">

At the start of a story the past continuous describes the situation. The
events that follow switch to the past simple.

- *Last night, I was walking home listening to my iPod when…*
- *The sun was shining, and lots of tourists were lying on the beach. Suddenly…*
- *It was getting dark, and I was walking fast. Suddenly…*

</Rule>

<Rule id="interrupted" title="A longer action interrupted by a shorter one" hint="The long background action is past continuous; the short event that interrupts it is past simple. Two past simples mean the actions happened one after the other.">

The past continuous is the longer action or situation, and the past simple is
the shorter one that happens in the middle of it — often interrupting it.

- *He was playing football when he broke his arm.*
- *When I met Susan, she was having a drink at a terrace with a friend.*
- *After dinner, I went into the living room and saw that she was crying.*
- *We didn’t go out because it was raining.*

Two past simples instead mean the actions happened one after the other:

<Chart
  columns={["Sentence", "Meaning"]}
  rows={[
    ["When he arrived, she [was having] a shower.", "The shower started before he arrived."],
    ["When he arrived, she [had] a shower.", "The shower started after he arrived."],
  ]}
/>

</Rule>


## Past Perfect

The past perfect is an **earlier past**: something that had already happened
before the main event of the story. It looks back from a past moment the same
way the present perfect looks back from now.

<Chart
  labelColumn
  columns={["", "had + past participle"]}
  rows={[
    ["affirmative", "She [had left]. They [had already eaten]."],
    ["negative", "He [hadn’t finished]. We [hadn’t seen] it before."],
    ["question", "[Had] you [met] her before? How long [had] they [been] there?"],
  ]}
/>

<Rule id="earlier-past" title="Earlier single events" hint="had + past participle for an event or experience completed before the main past event. Markers: already, just, never, before, by the time.">

Use it for earlier events and experiences, or single actions completed before
the main past event.

- *When she opened the door, he had already left.*
- *I realised that I had been there before.*
- *When I met her, I had never been in a serious relationship.*
- *He noticed I had cleaned the car. It was smooth and shiny.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="how-much" title="How much or how many, earlier in the past" hint="A quantity or a count completed before a past moment takes past perfect simple, never continuous.">

To say **how much** or **how many** had been done by a past moment, use the
past perfect simple, not the continuous.

- *We had driven 500 miles, and we needed some rest.*
- *How many hours had he slept when you woke him up?*

</Rule>

<Rule id="duration-stative" title="Duration with stative verbs" hint="Stative verbs (be, know, have, hate, love…) take no -ing, so a state lasting up to a past moment is past perfect simple with for, since, how long or always.">

Stative verbs (*be*, *know*, *have*, *hate*, *love*…) do not take *-ing*, so a
state that had started earlier and lasted up to a past moment is in the past
perfect **simple**, usually with *how long*, *for*, *since* or *always*.

- *The day Anne died, they had been married for 48 years.*
- *The day I left, I had been in England for exactly four years.*
- *She told me she had always hated her sister.*

</Rule>


## Past Perfect Continuous

The past perfect continuous is the earlier past with **duration**: a longer or
repeated action that started before the main event and ran up to it. It is
only for dynamic verbs; states use the past perfect simple.

<Chart
  labelColumn
  columns={["", "had been + -ing"]}
  rows={[
    ["affirmative", "I [had been waiting] for an hour. They [had been driving] all night."],
    ["negative", "She [hadn’t been sleeping] well."],
    ["question", "How long [had] you [been living] there?"],
  ]}
/>

<Rule id="duration-dynamic" title="Duration with dynamic verbs" hint="had been + -ing for a longer action that started earlier and ran up to the main past event; it often explains a state or a result at that moment.">

Use it with dynamic verbs for longer continuous actions that started earlier in
the past than the main events of the story. Very often it explains **why**
something was the case at that moment: red eyes, a tired voice, a broken car.

- *I was furious. I had been waiting for him in the cold, and he didn’t call to say he’d be late.*
- *We had been driving for less than an hour when the car broke down.*
- *I knew he had been crying because his eyes were red.*

</Rule>

<Rule id="repeated-earlier" title="Repeated actions from earlier in the past" hint="had been + -ing also covers something done again and again over a period before the past moment.">

It also covers actions repeated over a period before the past moment.

- *I couldn’t believe it. She had been writing a letter every day for over a year.*

</Rule>


## Choosing between them

Take one past moment, *when he arrived*, and ask what the other action was doing
relative to it. Each tense answers a different question.

<Chart
  labelColumn
  columns={["", "Example", "What it tells you"]}
  rows={[
    ["past simple", "When he arrived, she [had] a shower.", "It happened after — events in order."],
    ["past continuous", "When he arrived, she [was having] a shower.", "It was in progress at that moment."],
    ["past perfect", "When he arrived, she [had had] a shower.", "It was already finished before that moment."],
    ["past perfect continuous", "When he arrived, she [had been having] a shower for an hour.", "It had been going on for a while — and explains the wet hair."],
  ]}
/>

Words that usually point at a tense:

<Chart
  labelColumn
  columns={["", "Typical markers", "Example"]}
  rows={[
    ["past continuous", "while, as, at 9 o’clock, all day, when + past simple", "While I [was cooking], the phone rang."],
    ["past perfect", "already, just, never, before, by the time, after", "By the time we got there, the film [had started]."],
    ["past perfect continuous", "for, since, how long, all night + a dynamic verb", "He was tired because he [had been working] all night."],
  ]}
/>
